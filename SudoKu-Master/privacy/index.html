SudoKu Master Privacy Policy

Published by 1609007 B.C. Ltd., the company behind SudoKu Master.
Applies to SudoKu Master for iPhone and iPad.
Effective date: TBD (the day version 1.1.0 is submitted)

The short version: the app itself collects nothing about you and we run no server. Everything it remembers stays on your device. The one exception: from version 1.1.0 the app includes Google's ad software for optional rewarded ads; what Google receives is described under Ads. Subscribers never see an ad.

What we collect

Nothing is sent to us. We do not operate a server, and the app does not upload your data anywhere. If you use Report a problem in Settings, the app opens your own mail app with a pre-filled message (app version and build, platform, device model, operating system version, app language, and the difficulty, level and puzzle ID of the saved game); you can read and edit it, and nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself.

The app saves the following on your device only, so the game can pick up where you left off:

  • Your game in progress — the board, your pencil notes, elapsed time and mistake count.
  • Completed puzzle records — difficulty, level, how long it took, and the date.
  • Progress and achievements — unlocked lessons and techniques, Puzzle Journey progress, streaks, badges, and daily free-use counts (puzzles, hints, Auto Notes, practice).
  • Your settings — appearance and colours, language, sound and haptics, and the play-style rules.
  • Your subscription status — see below.

This data never leaves the device. Deleting the app deletes it. We cannot see it, recover it, or transfer it to a new device for you.

Purchases

From version 1.1.0, SudoKu Master offers an optional auto-renewing subscription, SudoKu Master Pro, monthly or yearly, sold through Apple's App Store.

  • Apple handles the transaction, not us. Payment, billing, renewals and cancellation happen between you and Apple. We never receive your name, email address, card number or billing address, and we never see your Apple Account.
  • We have no purchase server. The app asks the App Store directly whether the subscription is active, and trusts the store's own signed answer.
  • Subscription status is cached on your device. The app stores the identifiers of the products you own (for example the yearly plan) locally, so your access still works when you are offline. It is a short list of product identifiers — no receipt, no account details. Every refresh overwrites it, so a subscription that has lapsed loses access again.

Apple gives us only aggregated sales and subscription reports. These count purchases; they do not identify individual customers. You can manage or cancel the subscription in your Apple Account settings, or from Settings → Manage Subscription inside the app.

Ads

From version 1.1.0, free players can choose to watch a short rewarded ad for one more hint, one more use of Auto Notes, or the next practice level once the day's free uses are gone. The ads come from Google AdMob (Google LLC). There are no banner ads, no ads between games and no ads at launch, and an ad plays only after you tap a button that says it will. Subscribers are never offered an ad.

  • Non-personalised only. Every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads, chosen from context rather than from a profile of you.
  • No tracking. The app never asks for permission to track you and never reads your device's advertising identifier (IDFA). It does not share anything with data brokers.
  • What Google receives. From version 1.1.0, Google's ad software runs inside the app. When the app starts, it checks with Google whether your region needs your consent, and it starts Google's software; it loads an ad when a screen that offers one appears. Google can receive device and usage information such as your IP address (from which it derives an approximate location), device and operating-system type, an app-scoped device identifier, interactions with the ads, and crash and performance diagnostics. Google uses this to deliver and measure ads and to prevent fraud. How Google handles it is set out in Google's own policy: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Consent. Where Google's consent tool requires your consent (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), its message appears the first time you tap a "watch an ad" button, not when the app opens. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices, which appears wherever the consent tool requires it.

Photos

When you choose Save Image from the share sheet of a finished board, the app asks for permission to add photos to your library and saves that one picture. It cannot read, browse or delete anything already in your library.

Data we do not collect

  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, no login, no email address, no profile.
  • No location permission. The app never asks for your location. (From version 1.1.0, Google's ad software derives an approximate location from your IP address; see Ads.)
  • No contacts, camera or microphone, and no access to the photos already in your library.
  • No analytics or crash reporting of our own. There is no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK of ours in the app.
  • No tracking. The app does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not ask for the advertising identifier.

Apart from the ad software described above, the only links that leave the app are ones you tap yourself — the credits and legal links, the App Store review page, and Report a problem — and those open in your browser, the App Store or your mail app.

Children

SudoKu Master is a puzzle game suitable for all ages. The app itself collects no personal information from anyone. The optional ads (from version 1.1.0) are limited to content rated for general audiences and are always non-personalised. There is no chat, no user-generated content, and no way for a player to share information with other players through the app.

Changes to this policy

We update this policy before a version of the app that changes what it does with data is released, and record every revision in the table below. The privacy link inside the app always points to this page, which is the current policy.
